# Port KCC's page logic, not bindery's plumbing

Parallax should reimplement a small, well-defined slice of Kindle Comic Converter (KCC) in Rust and take only test cases and a few input-handling ideas from bindery. Bindery is an MIT-licensed Python/Docker "hot folder" that shells out to kepubify and KCC and does no image work of its own. KCC is the real engine, and its Kobo-relevant core (spread detection, crop, autocontrast, resize, palette mapping, spine spread pinning, ComicInfo reading) is about 1,800–2,000 lines of Python under a mixed ISC and GPL-3.0-or-later licence that a GPL-3.0-only app can port with attribution. The Libra 2 spike already settles the biggest question: **page size, not the container, makes the Kobo slow**, so processing pages to the screen should be the default. It also exposes the one design choice the spike cannot settle alone, which is spreads: processed versions split them, unprocessed ones showed them whole. KCC's three spread modes map directly onto the setting the spike asked for. The container (CBZ vs KEPUB) is still open, because titles, series, right-to-left page turns, ComicInfo and progress were not checked on the device. Parallax already has the pieces that matter most for that decision, such as direct `KoboReader.sqlite` writes, and is well ahead of both tools on library and device state.

## Bindery is an orchestration shell with about a day of useful logic

Bindery is about 2,150 lines of Python 3.13 in four modules, plus a 1,328-line template and roughly 2,200 lines of pytest. It runs Flask under one gunicorn worker, watches `Books_in` and `Comics_in` folders, and converts by calling `kcc-c2e` (pinned at KCC 11.0.1), `kepubify` 4.0.4 and `7z` ([Dockerfile](https://github.com/jarynclouatre/bindery/blob/730dd98/Dockerfile), [processor.py](https://github.com/jarynclouatre/bindery/blob/730dd98/processor.py#L712-L849)). It has **no library, metadata lookup, series database, cover work, device detection or device writes**; its "Kobo support" is producing KEPUB files that the user moves by hand ([README](https://github.com/jarynclouatre/bindery/blob/730dd98/README.md)). It is **MIT, copyright 2026 jarynclouatre**, so Parallax may copy or translate it if it keeps the MIT notice ([LICENSE](https://github.com/jarynclouatre/bindery/blob/730dd98/LICENSE)). It is young (created March 2026, about 205 commits, 67 stars) with a bus factor of one ([GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/jarynclouatre/bindery)).

The parts worth adapting are small. Its kepubify command builder exposes options Parallax ignores (`--no-add-dummy-titlepage`, hyphenation, smart punctuation, fullscreen fixes, CSS) and always passes values in `--opt=value` form so a leading dash cannot be read as a switch ([processor.py L808-L849](https://github.com/jarynclouatre/bindery/blob/730dd98/processor.py#L808-L849)); Parallax's `Kepubify::convert` in `crates/convert/src/lib.rs` passes only `--output`. Its chapter bundling natural-sorts a folder of chapter archives, extracts each into a zero-padded `NNN - stem` folder, and "hoists" single-folder wrappers until the images sit directly inside ([processor.py L1111-L1162](https://github.com/jarynclouatre/bindery/blob/730dd98/processor.py#L1111-L1162)). Its `_is_bundle_folder` gives a tested definition of when a folder is one volume: images only means yes, archives only means yes when bundling is on, anything mixed with PDFs or loose images means convert per file ([processor.py L1075-L1108](https://github.com/jarynclouatre/bindery/blob/730dd98/processor.py#L1075-L1108)). That suits scanlation downloads, which arrive one CBZ per chapter. Its UI also doubles as a current map of KCC's Kobo profile codes, with corrections such as `KoCE` becoming `KoCC` ([CHANGELOG](https://github.com/jarynclouatre/bindery/blob/730dd98/CHANGELOG.md)).

Bindery's changelog is more valuable than its code. It records the bugs a comic pipeline actually hits: cropping silently disabled because a percentage was passed where KCC wanted a ratio, dash-leading filenames breaking 7z, files picked up mid-copy, and library managers touching timestamps and causing endless re-conversion ([CHANGELOG](https://github.com/jarynclouatre/bindery/blob/730dd98/CHANGELOG.md)). Some of its own heuristics are weaker than Parallax's. Its raw-folder zipper sorts pages by lowercase name, not naturally, so `2.jpg` lands after `10.jpg` ([raw_processor.py](https://github.com/jarynclouatre/bindery/blob/730dd98/raw_processor.py#L72-L140)). Its ledger treats a same-size, mtime-only change as unchanged, which misses a same-length ComicInfo edit ([processor.py L200-L226](https://github.com/jarynclouatre/bindery/blob/730dd98/processor.py#L141-L226)); Parallax's SHA-256 identity is stronger. Its default deletes originals, which is wrong for a library app.

## KCC's page pipeline is the part worth porting

KCC's per-page order is fixed: load, detect background, crop margins and page numbers, detect and split spreads, then for each resulting page apply gamma, greyscale, autocontrast, resize, optional rainbow eraser, optional palette quantisation, and save ([comic2ebook.py L744-L777](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/blob/v13.0.0/kindlecomicconverter/comic2ebook.py#L744-L777)). Crop runs before the split, so each half of a spread gets its own autocontrast. Pages are natural-sorted with natsort's OS-style key and renamed `kcc-0001…` before processing ([comic2ebook.py L1246-L1280](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/blob/v13.0.0/kindlecomicconverter/comic2ebook.py#L1246-L1280)). The Libra 2 profile is just **1264×1680, a 16-level grey palette and gamma 1.0**; every Kobo profile uses gamma 1.0, so "auto gamma" is a no-op in v13 ([image.py L121-L137](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/blob/v13.0.0/kindlecomicconverter/image.py#L121-L137)). Colour Kobos have the same profile data as their mono siblings, and only the GUI turns on `--forcecolor` for them ([KCC_gui.py](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/blob/v13.0.0/kindlecomicconverter/KCC_gui.py#L1642-L1688), [issue #710](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/issues/710)).

Spread detection is purely aspect-based: a page is a spread when its orientation differs from the screen and width/height exceeds **1.16**. Below an aspect of **1.8** it is cut exactly at the midpoint, right half first in manga mode; at 1.8 or above it is rotated instead of split. Splitter mode 0 splits, mode 1 rotates, mode 2 emits both halves and then the rotated whole ([image.py L224-L281](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/blob/v13.0.0/kindlecomicconverter/image.py#L224-L281)). The CLI defaults to mode 0, but the GUI's first-run defaults are manga on and mode 2 ([KCC_gui.py L1534](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/blob/v13.0.0/kindlecomicconverter/KCC_gui.py#L1534)). With `--norotate`, whole spreads are sized to twice the screen width so zoom stays sharp ([image.py L591-L600](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/blob/v13.0.0/kindlecomicconverter/image.py#L591-L600), [issue #594](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/issues/594)). Parallax's spike matches KCC's `-m --splitter 0`.

Cropping is threshold-based bounding-box detection on an autocontrasted, box-blurred grey copy, with threshold `240 − 64·power`, edge-speck suppression, a page-number heuristic on the bottom 2.5% of the content box, and a hard cap of 10% per side ([page_number_crop_alg.py](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/blob/v13.0.0/kindlecomicconverter/page_number_crop_alg.py#L15-L185), [image.py L321-L350](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/blob/v13.0.0/kindlecomicconverter/image.py#L321-L350)). It is the largest port at about 350 lines, and it carries known problems: cropping a spread before splitting misaligns the halves ([issue #594](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/issues/594)), and a division-by-zero on zero-height images ([issue #1219](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/issues/1219)). Autocontrast is Pillow's min/max stretch with `preserve_tone`, skipped for pages whose range is under 159 ([image.py L538-L554](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/blob/v13.0.0/kindlecomicconverter/image.py#L538-L554)); it is the source of KCC's most persistent complaint, output darker than input ([issue #356](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/issues/356), [issue #982](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/issues/982)), which led to `--noautocontrast` ([issue #511](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/issues/511)). Resizing uses Lanczos down and bicubic up (only with `--upscale`), and pages within 1.5% of the screen's aspect are cropped to fill ([image.py L591-L635](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/blob/v13.0.0/kindlecomicconverter/image.py#L591-L635)).

Output encoding differs from the spike in one way that matters. **KCC's default is 8-bit JPEG at quality 85**; quantisation to 16 greys happens only on the opt-in PNG path, and there Pillow applies Floyd–Steinberg dithering by default before writing a 4-bit indexed PNG ([image.py L498-L584](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/blob/v13.0.0/kindlecomicconverter/image.py#L498-L584), [Pillow Image.py](https://github.com/python-pillow/Pillow/blob/main/src/PIL/Image.py)). KCC's README claims the 4-bit PNG gives better quality at half the size of JPEG ([README](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc#readme)). Parallax's spike used undithered 16-level PNG, and the user saw no loss, so dithering is an open device question, most visible on screentone and gradients, not a known requirement.

KCC's licence is mixed. The repository and `setup.py` say **ISC**, but `image.py`, inherited from Alex Yatskov's Mangle, carries a **GPL-3.0-or-later** header, and DualMetaFix is GPL-3 ([LICENSE.txt](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/blob/v13.0.0/LICENSE.txt), [image.py L1-L20](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/blob/v13.0.0/kindlecomicconverter/image.py#L1-L20)). The crop-algorithm files have no header and default to ISC. Both are compatible with GPL-3.0-only, provided Parallax keeps the copyright notices of the ported files. The icon is CC BY-NC-SA and must not be copied ([README](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc#copyright)). PDF input relies on PyMuPDF, which is AGPL-3.0 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/PyMuPDF/)).

## The Libra 2 result makes processing the default and spreads a setting

The spike separated container from processing: A is the original CBZ, B the original bytes repackaged as KEPUB, C a processed CBZ, D a processed KEPUB. On the Libra 2 (firmware 4.38) all four looked nearly identical, processed perhaps slightly better. **A and B turned pages in about 2 s; C and D turned almost instantly.** Because A and B share image bytes but not containers, and C and D share processing but not containers, the slowness belongs to the 1810×2560 JPEG pages, not to CBZ or KEPUB (`docs/kobo-comics-spike.md`). This fits Kobo's own publisher guidance of at most about **3.8 MP per fixed-layout page** ([kobolabs/epub-spec](https://github.com/kobolabs/epub-spec)): 1810×2560 is about 4.6 MP, and 1264×1680 is about 2.1 MP. Processing also cut three volumes from 1,501 MB to 369 MB and took about 8 s per volume on the Mac, so converting at send time with no cache is practical.

Processing has a cost, and the spike found it. A and B showed the opening spread whole on one screen; C and D showed it as two pages, so the reader never sees the picture at once. That is a genuine trade-off, not a bug, and KCC has already shaped it into three modes. "Split" (KCC mode 0) is fast and readable. "Keep whole" scales the unrotated spread to fit the portrait screen; it stays small and fast, which the spike's A/B display shows is what Nickel already does with a whole spread, minus the 2 s penalty. KCC reaches this only with `--norotate`, where it stores the spread at twice the screen width for zooming. "Both" emits the whole spread and then its halves. KCC's mode 2 uses a rotated whole spread instead, which suits a device held sideways; Parallax's spike doc asked for the unrotated whole first, which suits a reader who stays in portrait. KCC's 1.8 rule, rotating rather than splitting very wide images, is worth keeping in every mode, because splitting a panorama into two tall slivers reads badly.

Spine metadata interacts with this. The spike used `rendition:spread none`, whereas KCC writes `rendition:spread landscape` and pins split halves to `page-spread-right`/`left` with a backward alignment pass so halves face each other in landscape two-up ([comic2ebook.py L414-L503](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/blob/v13.0.0/kindlecomicconverter/comic2ebook.py#L305-L516)); that pass was fixed after a Libra Colour report of half spreads ([issue #930](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/issues/930)). Kobo's spec recommends `none` for books with hard-coded two-page spreads and `auto` or `landscape` for most others ([kobolabs/epub-spec](https://github.com/kobolabs/epub-spec)). So "keep whole" should use `none`, and "split" can use `landscape` with pinning if landscape two-up proves useful on the device.

## The container decision waits on metadata, not speed

With speed settled, CBZ vs KEPUB turns on what the spike did not report: title, author, series grouping, right-to-left turns, ComicInfo, and progress and stats. Community evidence leans toward KEPUB. A 2024 Libra 2 user's CBZ showed "Unknown Author" despite edited ComicInfo, and a KCC maintainer said CBZ metadata on Kobo "is not as easy as epub"; the KCC KEPUB showed correct title, author and table of contents ([MobileRead](https://www.mobileread.com/forums/showthread.php?p=4482947)). RTL for CBZ reportedly can only be set in the device database after import ([goncalor gist](https://gist.github.com/goncalor/aef56914c8a72faa3d056aadaeb5fab5)). One report says ComicInfo inside a CBZ broke Kobo's CBZ reader ([mihon#664](https://github.com/mihonapp/mihon/issues/664)), and KCC strips it from output for that reason ([comic2ebook.py L1185-L1189](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/blob/v13.0.0/kindlecomicconverter/comic2ebook.py#L1125-L1197)); that claim is anecdotal, and variant C tests it directly. On the other side, Kobo's spec says sideloaded `.kepub.epub` files **disable bookmarks and notes** ([kobolabs/epub-spec](https://github.com/kobolabs/epub-spec)), confirmed by open KCC issues ([kcc#704](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/issues/704), [kcc#418](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/issues/418)), though manga readers rarely need them.

Two facts tilt Parallax toward its current KEPUB route if the device confirms it. First, KCC's "KEPUB" is only a renamed fixed-layout EPUB; a KCC maintainer said renaming "won't work according to kepub spec" and pointed to kepubify ([kcc#418](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/issues/418)). Parallax's spike already runs kepubify and verified that it preserved RTL, fixed layout and series metadata, which is a superset of KCC. Whether kepubify's `koboSpan`s change progress or stats for an image-only book is unknown. Second, Parallax writes `KoboReader.sqlite` directly, so it could set `Series`, `SeriesNumber` and `PageProgressDirection` for a CBZ after import, as calibre's driver and Kobo Utilities do. That would neutralise CBZ's metadata weakness, but it is unverified on firmware 4.38. Series grouping for any sideloaded format is shaky on stock firmware: NickelSeries reads EPUB 3 `belongs-to-collection` ([NickelSeries](https://github.com/pgaskin/kobo-mods/tree/master/NickelSeries)), KCC added that field for it ([kcc#1186](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/issues/1186)), and sideloaded books reportedly get a `SeriesID` equal to the series name, which can split a series ([MobileRead](https://www.mobileread.com/forums/showthread.php?p=4482385)). Finally, a `!` anywhere in a KEPUB's path crashes the Kobo, which is why KCC sanitises file and folder names ([kcc#919](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/issues/919)); Parallax's `device_file_name` in `crates/device-kobo/src/transfer.rs` should be checked against that rule.

## Recommendations for Parallax

**Default pipeline.** Process every manga page by default: decode, greyscale (Rec. 601 luma, as Pillow's `convert('L')`), detect spreads with KCC's 1.16 and 1.8 thresholds, resize to the model's screen with Lanczos3 (no upscaling by default), map to 16 grey levels, and write 4-bit PNG, which the spike validated on the device. Keep autocontrast and margin cropping off by default and offer them as options, because the spike looked as good without them and both are KCC's main sources of complaints. Add a per-model screen-size table next to the model ids in `crates/device-kobo/src/version.rs`, using KCC's Kobo resolutions ([image.py L121-L137](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/blob/v13.0.0/kindlecomicconverter/image.py#L121-L137)), and fall back to Libra 2 size for unknown models. Convert at send time and store nothing on the Mac, as the spike decided. Treat colour Kobos as greyscale until tested.

**Spread handling.** Offer three choices: "Split" (right half first for RTL), "Keep whole" (unrotated, scaled to fit, `rendition:spread none`), and "Whole, then halves". Rotate rather than split anything at aspect 1.8 or wider. Make the default a device decision: "Split" is the readable default KCC ships on the CLI, but the user noticed the lost whole spread, so "Whole, then halves" is a strong candidate. Do not crop before splitting, which avoids KCC's misaligned halves.

**Container.** Keep kepubify-based KEPUB as the working default, because it already preserves RTL and series metadata, and keep processed CBZ as an option. Do not copy KCC's rename-only KEPUB. Before committing, finish the spike checklist for title, author, series grouping, RTL page turns, ComicInfo in C, progress and stats, and also test whether Parallax's own database writes give a CBZ the same title, series and RTL. Still unknown: whether firmware 4.38 reads ComicInfo or OPF series natively, whether `koboSpan`s affect progress for image-only books, and whether landscape two-up with page pinning is worth supporting.

**CBR and licensing.** Do not link `unrar` or `unrar_sys`: the UnRAR licence forbids using it to re-create RAR compression ([unrar.rs license](https://github.com/muja/unrar.rs/blob/master/unrar_sys/vendor/unrar/license.txt)), an extra restriction that is widely treated as GPL-incompatible, which is why Debian ships it in non-free. Read CBR through libarchive via `compress-tools` (MIT/Apache-2.0 bindings) and convert to CBZ on import, so only the importer needs RAR support. libarchive's RAR5 coverage and how to bundle it in a Tauri macOS app were not researched. Record ported code in a third-party notice: MIT for bindery, ISC for KCC's crop and metadata files, and GPL-3.0-or-later with the Mangle authors' copyright for anything derived from `image.py`. Skip PDF input, which would pull in AGPL PyMuPDF or another heavy renderer.

**Crates.** Use `zip` (already in the spike) for CBZ and EPUB, `image` for decode and encode, `fast_image_resize` for SIMD Lanczos3, `rayon` for per-page parallelism, `quick-xml` for ComicInfo read and write, and `compress-tools` for CBR and CB7 ([crates.io data in KCC notes](https://crates.io/crates/compress-tools)). Write the fixed-palette mapping (with optional Floyd–Steinberg) and natural sort by hand; each is a few dozen lines, `imagequant` is unnecessary for a fixed palette, and `natord` has not been released since 2015 ([crates.io](https://crates.io/crates/natord)). `oxipng` is optional if PNG size matters. Avoid `epub-builder`, since the spike's hand-built OPF already does more, and the abandoned `libarchive` and superseded `sevenz-rust` crates.

**Code to port or adapt, in order.** Spread detection, split and rotate with RTL order (about half a day); natural sort for pages and chapter archives; ComicInfo read and write, with KCC's `Series Vol. NN #NNN` title rule as a fallback when the library has no metadata ([comic2ebook.py L1155-L1178](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/blob/v13.0.0/kindlecomicconverter/comic2ebook.py#L1125-L1197)); bindery's volume classification and chapter bundling with wrapper hoisting; kepubify options in `crates/convert` (at least `--no-add-dummy-titlepage`, passed as `--opt=value`); KCC's spine page-spread pinning if landscape spreads are adopted; and margin and page-number cropping last, behind golden-image tests. CBZ import into the library (currently EPUB-only, `crates/library/src/lib.rs`) is a prerequisite that neither tool helps with.

**What not to port.** From bindery: the Flask UI, Docker image, watcher loops, Apprise, the delete-originals default and the size-plus-mtime ledger. From KCC: the Qt GUI, MOBI, KindleGen, DualMetaFix, KFX, panel view and Kindle-only OPF metas, PDF input and output, the 200 MB tome split (Kobo's documented limit is 1 GB per EPUB), the 7-Zip `!mimetype` hack, and the rename-only KEPUB. Defer the FFT rainbow eraser until colour Kobos are tested and the webtoon panel splitter until webtoons matter.

**Test cases worth taking.** From bindery's suite ([tests/test_processor.py](https://github.com/jarynclouatre/bindery/blob/730dd98/tests/test_processor.py)), turned into Rust tests with small generated CBZ fixtures: chapters ordered naturally (`ch2` before `ch10`); unpadded page numbers (`2.jpg` before `10.jpg`, the case bindery's raw zipper gets wrong); nested single-folder wrappers hoisted; dot-dirs, `__MACOSX`, `.DS_Store`, `Thumbs.db` and `desktop.ini` ignored; folders mixing PDFs or loose images with archives handled per file; empty and non-comic folders ignored; dash-leading file names; kepubify flag mapping, including omitted null options; and temporary output cleaned up on failure. Invert bindery's timestamp-only test: a same-size content change must trigger re-conversion, which SHA-256 identity guarantees. Add from KCC: zip-slip entries (`..` and absolute paths) rejected, a zero-height or truncated image that does not panic ([issue #1219](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/issues/1219)), a `!` in the title or folder sanitised ([kcc#919](https://github.com/ciromattia/kcc/issues/919)), spreads at aspects just below and above 1.16 and 1.8, and RTL half order.

## Conclusion

The two projects look like competitors to Parallax's manga plan but are better read as a specification and a bug list. KCC encodes fifteen years of decisions about thresholds, spread modes and spine pinning that Parallax can reimplement in a few thousand lines of Rust, and bindery's changelog lists the input-handling failures Parallax would otherwise rediscover. Neither touches the device, which is where Parallax's advantage lies: it can fix CBZ's metadata gaps through the Kobo database rather than through file formats, an option no converter has.

The spike also reframes the work. KCC's many image options suggest quality is the hard part, but on a Libra 2 the visible differences were negligible and the decisive variable was pixel count. That means the first release needs only resize, greyscale, 16 levels and a spread setting, and everything heavier (cropping, autocontrast, dithering, colour) should earn its place through device tests rather than parity with KCC. The remaining risk is not image quality but metadata: one more pass through the checklist decides whether Parallax ships KEPUB, CBZ plus database writes, or both.
